How Long Does a Made to Measure Wedding Dress Really Take, Month by Month?

A wedding dress made to measure takes one to six months here, depending on the model, the design and the cloth. Most of that time is not sewing. It is the toile, the fittings and the settling between them. Across the industry, lead times of twelve to twenty weeks are normal, so write early if your date is fixed.

The question I get most often is not what a dress costs. It is whether there is still time. So here is the honest shape of it.

Why does it take months if the sewing takes weeks?

Because a wedding dress is made in rounds, and rounds need gaps.

You come in, I fit, I mark, you leave, I work, you come back. Each of those gaps is real work on my table and it is also time for the piece to settle. Cloth relaxes. A hem hung for a week is not the hem it was on the day it was pinned. Rushing that is how a dress ends up looking almost right.

There is also a limit on how many commissions are in the atelier at once. That is a choice, and it is why I ask when you need the piece before I ask anything about the design.

What happens in month one?

We talk, and I measure.

The first conversation is free and nothing is decided in it. We go through the date, the venue, how you want to feel and move, whether you are getting married in a church in February or on a coast in July, and what you already know you do not want. That last list is usually the most useful thing you bring.

Then measurements, then a written quotation with the price, what is included and when the piece will be ready. Work starts once you accept it. Until then there is no agreement and no cost.

What happens in month two?

The toile. This is the month that decides the dress.

I cut a full version of the gown in plain cotton and we fit it, mark it and take it apart. For a wedding dress this often happens more than once, because a bodice that has to hold a body upright for fourteen hours is not something you get right in one pass. You will sit down in it, raise your arms in it, and walk in it in the shoes you plan to wear.

This is also the last moment where changing your mind is free. A neckline, a back, the weight of a skirt: all of that can move at the toile stage without touching the price or the date. The five steps are set out here.

What happens in months three and four?

The real cloth is cut, and the fittings begin.

A couture piece gets three fittings as part of the price. A wedding dress usually asks for more, and any extra fitting is agreed and priced before it happens, never afterwards. Each time, the garment moves closer to your body: a strap shortened by half a centimetre, a waist seam that wants to sit higher, a back that needs to close differently now that the boning is in.

If there is hand work, this is where it lives. Lace applied by hand, tulle gathered by hand, a bodice built and rebuilt. Hand work is slow by definition and it is most of what people are actually paying for.

What happens in the last month?

The slow work, which is the work nobody notices unless it is wrong.

Hems. Closures. Linings. The inside of the dress, which is the part that decides whether you can breathe in it. A final fitting close to the date, so that the dress fits the body that will wear it and not the body from four months earlier.

Then the handover. You collect the finished piece at the atelier and try it on one last time before it leaves.

Can it be done in less time?

Sometimes. One month is the bottom of my range and it is not the comfortable path.

A short timeline means fewer rounds, a simpler construction, and cloth that is already available rather than ordered in. It can be done and I have done it. What I will not do is take a date that is too close to do the work properly and sell you a rush. You will be told instead. That is written into the terms.

If the date is very close, say so in the first line of your message. It changes what I propose, not whether I answer.

Is a custom dress still unusual?

Less than it used to be. Made to order has moved from the edge of the bridal market to the middle of it.

VerityRank's bridal market analysis puts the global bridal wear market above 35 billion dollars in 2026, and reports that 78 percent of collections at New York Bridal Fashion Week now offer made to order or semi-custom options, against 45 percent in 2022. The same analysis puts over 60 percent of bridal gowns in the made to order category, with lead times of twelve to twenty weeks.

What that tells you is useful and slightly misleading at once. Made to order at a house usually means an existing pattern produced in your size with some choices attached. That is a real service and it is not what happens here. Here the pattern is drawn from your body, used once, and kept for you. The difference between those two things is worth understanding before you start shopping, and it is not a difference most price lists explain.

When should I write if my date is two years away?

Now, if you want to.

Dresses are talked about long before they are made. There is nothing to decide two years out and no reason to hold a date. But I would rather hear from you early than late, and the conversation costs nothing. You can see what bridal work looks like here, or just tell me your date and let it sit.

Questions people ask

How long does a made to measure wedding dress take? One to six months from the first conversation to the finished dress, depending on the model, the design and the cloth. Twelve to sixteen weeks is a comfortable middle for most gowns.

How many fittings does a wedding dress need? More than a couture piece. Three fittings are included in the price, and a wedding dress usually asks for more. Extra fittings are agreed and priced before they happen.

What is the latest I can start? One month is the bottom of the range, and it limits the design and the cloth. If a date is too close to do the work properly, you will be told so rather than sold a rush.

Do I need to live in Amsterdam? No. The atelier is in Amsterdam and fittings happen there by appointment, but early conversations can be online and clients travel in for fittings.

What if I have not chosen anything yet? That is the normal starting point. Bring the date and a rough feeling. The design is what the first conversation is for.
